TiltFlow

Tilt your phone. The page scrolls itself.

TiltFlow scrolls the page for you when you tilt your phone gently to one side, so you can read one-handed without swiping. Tilt one way to move forward, the other way to go back, and return to your normal reading position to stop. The further you tilt, the faster it scrolls.

Only where you want it

TiltFlow does nothing until you say where it should work. Choose your browser, your e-book reader, your PDF application — and TiltFlow stays completely inactive everywhere else. Open your messages or the camera and it switches itself off. Go back to reading and it comes back on, without you doing anything.

Made to fit how you read

  • Calibrate your own neutral position, so it works however you hold your phone.
  • Set how far you must tilt before scrolling starts, and how far to reach full speed.
  • Swap the tilt directions if you prefer them the other way round.
  • Move the invisible swipe left, centre or right for applications that need it.

E-book readers

Readers such as Kindle turn whole pages sideways rather than scrolling. TiltFlow handles those too: enable Turn pages sideways and One swipe per tilt for that application, and one tilt turns exactly one page, however long you hold it.

Privacy

TiltFlow does not read the content of your screen, and cannot send anything anywhere.

The released application requests no Android permissions at all — including no internet permission — so it has no way to transmit information off your phone. There is no account, no advertising and no analytics.

Full details are in the TiltFlow privacy policy.

How it works

TiltFlow measures sideways tilt with your phone's motion sensor, and uses Android's Accessibility Service to perform the same swipe your finger would. Android only allows an application to swipe on your behalf through that service, which is why TiltFlow asks you to enable it — and why it explains what the service does before sending you to the settings screen.

You can turn it off at any time, in TiltFlow or in Android's Accessibility settings.

Compatibility

TiltFlow works with applications that respond to ordinary swipe gestures. Some applications — certain games, and readers with copy protection — ignore simulated swipes and cannot be scrolled this way.

Requires Android 7.0 or later.
